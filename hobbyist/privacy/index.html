Hobbyist Privacy Policy

Effective 24 November 2026 · Applies to the Hobbyist app for Android (com.svarosystems.hobbyist)

Hobbyist ("the app") is published by Oleksandr Kovban, Ukraine (UA) ("we"). This policy explains what the app does with your data. If you have a question, write to support.hobbyist@gmail.com.

In short

  • Your hobbies, records, events, notes and photos are stored only on your phone. We have no server that receives them, and there is no account or sign-in.
  • Crash reports and usage counts are sent only if you allow them, and you can turn them off any time in Settings › Privacy. They never contain your names, notes or photos.
  • If you use location features, the app sends an approximate position to a weather service and a map service to show you weather and maps.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, or use the advertising ID.

1. Data that stays on your phone

Everything you enter in the app (hobbies, objects, events, records, descriptions, metadata such as dates, numbers, GPS positions and weather, and the photos you add) is stored in the app's private storage on your device. We cannot see it.

  • Photos. When you add a photo, the app takes it with the camera or lets you pick it from your gallery, and keeps a copy in its own storage. The app sees only the photos you pick.
  • Backups you make. A backup is a .zip file with your data and photos. It goes only where you choose to save or share it (for example a folder on the phone, Google Drive or email).
  • Sharing. When you share a record, an object or a summary, the app creates an image or text and passes it to the app you pick. What happens next is governed by that app's privacy policy.
  • Android backup. If backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may include the app's data in your device backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps. This is controlled by your phone's settings and Google's privacy policy, not by us.

To delete this data, delete it in the app, or uninstall the app, which removes everything stored in its private storage.

2. Crash reports and usage counts (only with your permission)

After you save your first record, the app asks whether you allow crash reports and usage counts. Until you answer, nothing is sent. You can change either choice any time in Settings › Privacy.

We use Google Firebase, a service of Google, for both:

  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics). When the app crashes or hits an error: the error and where in the code it happened, the app version, the device model, the Android version, and a random installation identifier.
  • Usage counts (Google Analytics for Firebase). How often a few things happen: a hobby created, a record added, photos added, something shared (and to which app), a backup made or restored. Firebase also records standard app events such as the first open, app sessions and updates, together with the device model, Android version, app version, language, approximate country or region (derived from your IP address) and a random app instance identifier.

These never include the names, descriptions, notes, metadata values or photos you enter. We do not set a user identifier, do not collect the advertising ID, and have turned off ad personalisation. We use this data only to fix problems and understand which features are used.

Google keeps crash reports for 90 days and usage data for up to 14 months. See Privacy and Security in Firebase and the Google Privacy Policy.

3. Location

The app asks for location permission only when you first use a feature that needs it, such as filling in a GPS position on an entry. It uses location only while you are using the app, never in the background. You can refuse or withdraw the permission in Android's settings; you can still type a position or pick it on a map.

The position is saved with your entry on your phone. To show you related information, the app sends positions to these services:

ServiceWhat it receivesWhy
MET Norway (Norwegian Meteorological Institute) The position rounded to about 1 km, your IP address, and the app's name and version To fill in the weather when you add or edit an entry with a position
OpenStreetMap Which map area you view, your IP address, and the app's name To show map images when you view or pick a position on a map
Your phone's geocoding service (on most Android phones provided by Google) The position To show a place name, such as a town, for a position

When you choose to open a position in a maps app or browser, that position is passed to the app you open.

4. Other services

  • Fonts. The app downloads its typefaces once from Google Fonts. Google receives your IP address and the name of the font, not any of your data.
  • Ratings. If the app asks you to rate it, the rating dialog is shown and handled by Google Play.

5. What we don't do

  • No accounts, sign-in or profile.
  • No advertising and no use of the advertising ID.
  • No selling or renting of data, and no sharing of data for advertising.
  • No access to your contacts, messages or other apps' files.

6. Your rights

Your hobby data is under your control on your phone: you can view, change, export (back up) and delete it at any time. Crash reports and usage counts are not linked to your name or any account, so we generally cannot find the data that belongs to one person. You can stop them at any time in Settings › Privacy. Depending on where you live (for example in the EU, the UK or California), you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal data and to complain to a data protection authority. To exercise these rights, contact us at the address below.

7. Children

Hobbyist is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them.

8. Security

Your data stays in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Data sent to the services above travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Backup files you create are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere safe.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles data, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches the app. Future features such as cloud backup or accounts will be described here before they are released.
